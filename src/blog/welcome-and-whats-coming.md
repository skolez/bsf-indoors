BSF Indoors is a working build, not a finished site. The colony is running
right now — prepupae self-harvested earlier this week, the first cohort of
adults will eclose over the next 10–14 days, and the love cage is getting
its first real test. Articles drop as the cycle does.

## What this site is

A primary-source reference for keeping a black soldier fly larvae colony
indoors **year-round** in a cold climate. The operator runs the rig in
Cache Valley, Utah (USDA Zone 5b, ~4,500 ft, sub-zero winters). The whole
project exists because most BSF resources quietly assume Floridian or
sub-tropical conditions, and what works there doesn't always work in a
basement at 4,500 ft.

## What's on the docket (next 6 weeks)

These are the articles being captured live as the colony runs its first
documented full cycle. Each one is paired with photos from the rig and
cited against the [primary source catalog](https://github.com/skolez/bsf-indoors/blob/main/agents/sources.md).

1. **The indoor mating cage: 48×24×24 in a cold-climate basement.** Cage
   geometry, where to put it, how it interacts with humidity and the rest
   of the room.
2. **Why we removed the UV filter from a worklight (and the safety we added
   back).** What the bare quartz envelope actually does, the fire and
   UV-eye risks, distance-to-mesh math, and the thermostat circuit that
   stops a runaway.
3. **Self-harvesting prepupae: what we saw in week one.** The migration
   instinct, ramp angles, and what to do with the dark ones once they've
   walked off the food.
4. **Pupation chamber: bedding vs. no bedding.** What corrugated cardboard,
   coco coir, vermiculite, and "nothing at all" actually do for pupation
   success and ease of adult transfer.
5. **Indoor BSF in 28% humidity.** The Cache Valley dry-air problem —
   ambient room RH at 28%, cage target at 60%, and how to bridge the gap
   without wrecking the network rack in the same room.
6. **Light cycle: timer, thermostat, and why pulsing the lamp is a bad
   idea.** Photoperiod, halogen lifespan, and the 2-stage safety circuit.

## Why slow

The portfolio that BSF Indoors belongs to (alongside
[Cache Valley Bee Supply](https://cachevalleybees.com)) has one rule:
**no site launches on aggregated content alone.** Every article needs
firsthand material — photos from the rig, decisions actually made, and
failures actually had — anchoring it. That means we publish slower than a
content-mill site would, but the articles are worth reading and the gear
recommendations come from gear that actually got used.

The trade is intentional.
